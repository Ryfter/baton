What's irreducibly Baton

  1. Cost-optimal routing that learns from measured benchmarks of your private local fleet (Firefly, wraith2) with
  frontier as fallback. Neither project benchmarks local models or routes on per-job×context measured scores. This is
  your differentiated bet and the reason Baton exists.
  2. The decision + lessons KB (Grimdex), model-agnostic and GitHub-backed. Your operating memory across agents.

  Everything else in Baton is a reimplementation of what these two do better.

  Recommendation

  Don't switch wholesale, and don't keep building Baton as a full harness. Both lose. Instead:

  - Narrow Baton to its two crown jewels (the learned local-fleet router + the KB loop), packaged so they can ride on
  top of someone else's plumbing.
  - Adopt the plumbing layer you're tired of reinventing. The two aren't even competitors — they're different layers:
    - Omnigent = the runtime/conductor/cockpit (multi-harness, sessions, hosts, policies, multi-device). This is
  literally your parked Fleet-Conductor cockpit, already built, and it satisfies "orchestrate, don't solo" + the
  legibility north-star.
    - Archon = the process layer (declarative YAML workflows + gates + worktrees). Its DAG-as-source-of-truth would
  replace your bespoke phase hooks and PowerShell glue — the most thankless part of Baton.

  If I had to pick one to lean on first: Omnigent, because it's the closest match to your parked cockpit vision and to
  the conductor identity Baton already claims. Archon is the better grab if what's draining you is the imperative
  workflow glue rather than the multi-agent runtime.

  Two caveats that matter for you:
  - Maturity risk cuts both ways. Omnigent is alpha — adopting it as a foundation trades "my unfinished thing" for
  "their unfinished thing." Archon just archived its Python v1 and rewrote in TS, so it's also in flux. Don't build on
  either until you've kicked the tires for a week.
  - Box-private constraint survives this. Your local-fleet routing + private inventories stay local regardless —
  Omnigent's "hosts register on your local network" is compatible, and the router itself never has to leave your boxes.
  Verify that before committing.

  What to learn either way (even if you adopt neither)

  - From Archon: declarative workflows as source of truth beats imperative hooks. Even inside Baton, moving job/phase
  from PowerShell glue to a YAML DAG would make it finishable and legible.
  - From Omnigent: the Harness abstraction (treat Claude/Codex/Pi uniformly) and Policy as a first-class object (spend
  caps, approval gates) instead of scattered logic.

  My honest one-liner

  Redefine "done" for Baton as: "a cost-optimal, benchmark-learned router over my private fleet + my decision KB,
  exposed as a thin layer on top of a community harness." That's finishable in weeks and defensibly unique. The
  harness/cockpit/workflow ambitions are exactly what made it unfinishable — and they're now off the shelf.
